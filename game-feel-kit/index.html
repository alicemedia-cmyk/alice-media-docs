Quick start (Unreal Engine)

Game Feel Kit adds "juice" to any Unreal project: camera shake, hitstop, flashes, punches, vibration and floating numbers. Effects are bundled into presets and played with one node.

Requirements: Unreal Engine 5.6, 5.7 or 5.8, Windows 64-bit.

1. Play a built-in preset

In any Blueprint (for example, when an enemy takes damage):

Play Built In Feedback: - Preset: Hit Heavy - Target: the damaged actor - Intensity: 0..1 (a light hit 0.3, a heavy one 1) - Value: the damage, shown as a floating number

That is all. The camera kicks, the game freezes for a few frames, the enemy flashes and recoils, and the number pops above its head.

Demo map: Juice Lab

/GameFeelKit/Demo/Maps/L_GameFeelKit_JuiceLab (turn on Show Plugin Content in the Content Browser). Press Play: the director plays all 42 presets on three dummies with each preset's name on screen. - Left / Right pick a preset, Space plays it, Up / Down pick a dummy, A toggles autoplay. - Tab opens the accessibility panel (the same settings your players get), R applies Reduce Motion. - Console: GameFeelDemo.Play HitCritical, GameFeelDemo.Auto 0, GameFeelDemo.Panel 1, GameFeelDemo.Access ScreenFlash 0.3, GameFeelDemo.PlayWith HitHeavy 1 1 Hitstop.Duration=0.3, GameFeelDemo.Set MaxShakeRotation 8.

The map brings its own game mode (World Settings); your maps are not affected.

2. Make it yours

  1. Tools > Create Built-in Game Feel Presets saves all 42 presets as assets in /Game/GameFeelKit/Presets.
  2. Open one (for example DA_GFK_HitHeavy) and change values, or add effects with +.
  3. Use Play Feedback with your asset instead of the built-in one.

A new preset: Content Browser > Add > Miscellaneous > Game Feel Preset.

3. Tune while playing

Open Tools > Game Feel Timeline and press Play. Every preset that fires appears on the graph with its channels: shake, FOV, flash, time stop, filters, vibration. Replay plays it again on the same target. Edit the asset, press Replay, and compare.

4. Effects

Effect What it does
Camera Shake Noise, sine or directional kick, with distance falloff, no shake asset needed
FOV Kick Quick zoom in or out
Hitstop Freezes the game or only the target for a few frames
Slow Motion Time scale with an envelope
Screen Flash Color flash, protected by the Flash Guard
Screen Filter Vignette, chromatic aberration, desaturation, tint, pulse
Scale Punch Spring scale pop or squash and stretch that keeps the volume
Transform Punch Recoil along the hit direction with a wobble
Material Flash Material parameter or custom primitive data (the white hit flash)
Light Flash Short point light
Sound Random pitch, volume follows intensity
Niagara Burst Spawns a system, aligned to the hit
Vibration Procedural controller rumble, no asset needed
Floating Number Damage, critical, heal, miss; rapid hits merge
Text Pop "PERFECT!" on screen
Blueprint Event Your own logic

For the white hit flash, add a scalar parameter HitFlash to your material: lerp the base color to white by it.

5. Mixer and limits

Many effects at once stay readable: - Shakes, FOV kicks and filters are summed and soft-limited. - Lower priorities are ducked. - Hitstop has a budget per second, so a minigun never freezes the game.

Limits are in Project Settings > Plugins > Game Feel Kit.

6. Accessibility (ship it in your options menu)

Bind these nodes to your options menu: - Get Accessibility and Set Accessibility: sliders for camera shake, camera motion, screen flash, time effects, vibration and screen effects, plus a numbers on/off switch. - Apply Reduce Motion: one switch for motion-sensitive players.

The Flash Guard is on by default. It allows at most three flashes per second and dims saturated red flashes (WCAG 2.3.1).

Settings are saved per player in GameUserSettings.ini.

7. Multiplayer

Feedback is cosmetic and local. Play it on the client that should feel it; nothing runs on a dedicated server. For hitstop in multiplayer, use Scope: Target (only the hit actor freezes).

8. C++

#include "GameFeelBlueprintLibrary.h"
UGameFeelBlueprintLibrary::PlayFeedback(this, HitPreset, HitActor, 0.8f, Damage);

Custom effect: subclass UGameFeelEffect and override Start (return an FGameFeelInstance for effects that run over time).

Quick start (Unity)

Game Feel Kit adds "juice" to any Unity game: camera shake, hitstop, flashes, punches, vibration and floating numbers. Effects are bundled into presets and played with one call.

Requirements: Unity 6 (6000.6), any render pipeline. With URP, screen filters use a URP Volume; without it, a simple overlay. Vibration needs the Input System package.

1. Install

Package Manager > + > Add package from disk, then pick com.alicemedia.gamefeel/package.json.

Demo scene: Juice Lab

Package Manager > Game Feel Kit > Samples > Juice Lab (URP) > Import, open Scenes/GameFeel_JuiceLab_Day (or _Night) and press Play. Three robot dummies stand on a pier; the demo plays all 42 built-in presets with their names. Left / Right pick a preset, Space plays it, Up / Down pick a dummy, A toggles autoplay, Tab opens the accessibility panel, R applies Reduce Motion. The scene uses URP materials; the package itself works in the built-in pipeline too.

2. Play a built-in preset

When an enemy takes damage:

using AliceMedia.GameFeel;

GameFeel.Play(GameFeelBuiltIn.HitHeavy, enemy.gameObject, intensity: 1f, value: damage);

The camera kicks, the game freezes for a few frames, the enemy recoils and the number pops above it. - Intensity: a light hit 0.3, a heavy one 1. - Value: the damage shown. - Direction (optional): the hit direction, for the recoil and the camera kick.

No code: add Game Feel > Game Feel Player to the enemy, set a preset per name, and call Play Feedback ("Hit") from a UnityEvent.

3. Make it yours

  1. Tools > Game Feel > Create Built-in Presets saves all 42 presets as assets in Assets/GameFeelKit/Presets (existing assets are kept).
  2. Select one (for example GF_HitHeavy) and change values, or add effects with Add Effect. In Play Mode, Play on Selection plays it on the selected object.
  3. Play your asset: GameFeel.Play(myPreset, target, 1f, damage).

A new preset: Assets > Create > Game Feel > Preset.

4. Tune while playing

Open Window > Analysis > Game Feel Timeline and press Play. - Every preset that fires is listed, and its channels (shake, FOV, flash, time stop, filters, vibration) are drawn on a graph. - Replay plays it again on the same target.

5. Effects

Camera Shake, FOV Kick, Hitstop, Slow Motion, Screen Flash, Screen Filter, Scale Punch (squash and stretch that keeps the volume), Transform Punch, Material Flash, Light Flash, Sound, Particle Burst, Vibration, Floating Number, Text Pop, Event.

White hit flash on the material: add a float property _HitFlash to your shader (Shader Graph: a Float named _HitFlash, used to lerp the base color to white). The flash sets it through a MaterialPropertyBlock and resets it when it ends.

Camera: shake and FOV kicks are added to the main camera only while it renders and removed after, so they never fight your camera controller or Cinemachine. Set GameFeel.Camera to use another camera.

Time: - Hitstop and slow motion change Time.timeScale and put your game's own value back. A pause that sets timeScale during a hitstop is kept. - Hitstop Scope: Target freezes only the target's Animators and particle systems; use it in multiplayer.

6. Mixer and limits

  • Shakes, FOV kicks and filters are summed and soft-limited; lower priorities are ducked.
  • Hitstop has a budget per second, so a minigun never freezes the game.

Limits: Tools > Game Feel > Settings creates Assets/GameFeelKit/Resources/GameFeelSettings.asset.

7. Accessibility

Bind these to your options menu: - GameFeel.Accessibility and GameFeel.SetAccessibility(...): sliders for camera shake, camera motion, screen flash, time effects, vibration and screen effects, and a numbers switch. Saved per player in PlayerPrefs. - GameFeel.ReduceMotion(true): one switch for motion-sensitive players.

The Flash Guard is on by default. It allows at most three flashes per second and dims saturated red flashes (WCAG 2.3.1).

8. Stopping

  • var handle = GameFeel.Play(GameFeelBuiltIn.LowHealth);, later GameFeel.Stop(handle);. Held effects fade out; pass immediate: true to drop them at once.
  • GameFeel.StopAll() stops everything and puts time, materials and lights back.
